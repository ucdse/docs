# Interview Transcripts

## 1. Interview: Sarah (The Punctual Office Worker)

### Phase 1: Warm-up
**1. Interviewer: What is your primary mode of transport when moving around Dublin city center? (Bus, Luas, Walking, Cycling?)**
* **Sarah:** I take the train into the city first, but for the crucial "last mile" from the train station to my office in the Silicon Docks, the bike is my choice.

**2. Interviewer: How often do you use bike-sharing services like Dublin Bikes?**
* **Sarah:** Almost every single workday, typically twice a day. It’s an essential part of my daily routine.

### Phase 2: Current Habits & Pain Points
**3. Interviewer: What is the biggest reason you choose a bike over the bus or walking?**
* **Sarah:** It gives me more control over time than taking a bus or the Luas because of the uncertain waiting time.

**4. Interviewer: Have you ever walked to a bike station and found it empty? How did that affect your plans?**
* **Sarah:** Almost never. Since I am a P person, I do plan before every commute to make sure everything will go well, and nothing unexpected will emerge. But when I encountered such a situation, I had to take a taxi to arrive at my office on time.

**5. Interviewer: Have you ever arrived at your destination and found the bike station full? What did you do then?**
* **Sarah:** It happens. There are a lot of other commuters doing the same thing that I am doing. I check the historical statistics on the app and try to predict the vacancy status of a station before the trip. But sometimes it’s not working perfectly. In this situation, I had to find another station with available slots and return the bike. It makes me spend even more time than walking from the train station to the office, which can be so annoying.

**6. Interviewer: How does the Dublin weather influence your decision to rent a bike? Do you check a weather app before you leave?**
* **Sarah:** Yeah, the weather plays a central role in my decision-making process. Of course, nobody wants to ride a bike in the rain because of the slippery road and insufficient field of view. So I checked the weather forecast every time I thought of taking a bike. Sometimes I had to cancel the plan and choose another transportation mode.

### Phase 3: Feature Validation
**7. Interviewer: Real-time Info: If you could see exactly how many bikes are left at a station from your phone, would that change how you plan your walk?**
* **Sarah:** Absolutely yes. It could be so helpful that my plan can be improved and corrected to fit the known actual status of stations. Besides, I can also adjust my plan dynamically according to the real-time info.

**8. Interviewer: Journey Planning: When going somewhere new in Dublin, do you usually know the best cycling route, or do you use Google Maps?**
* **Sarah:** I use Google Maps and Apple Maps to organise my route if I need to arrive there as fast as possible. But sometimes it's not for commuting but for sightseeing, so I am also checking social media to get a popular cycling route.

**9. Interviewer: Parking Assurance: On a scale of 1 to 10, how important is it for you to know if there is an empty stand at your destination before you even start cycling?**
* **Sarah:** 8 out of 10. Returning a bike is an essential and inevitable procedure during the sharing bike process, which significantly depends on the availability of a station. No empty stand results in a big penalty: looking for another station, riding there, returning bikes, walking to office, wasting both time and money.

## 3. Interview: Eoin (The Daily Student Commuter)

### Phase 1: Warm-up
**1. Interviewer: What is your primary mode of transport when moving around Dublin city center?**
* **Eoin:** Cycling is my main choice. I prefer cycling over paying for the bus. I commute daily to UCD.

**2. Interviewer: How often do you use bike-sharing services like Dublin Bikes?**
* **Eoin:** Every day. I'm a daily commuter.

### Phase 2: Current Habits & Pain Points
**3. Interviewer: What is the biggest reason you choose a bike over the bus or walking?**
* **Eoin:** There are a few reasons. Primarily, I'm on a tight budget, and while I actually prefer the feel of riding my own bike, the risk of theft in the city is too high for me. I can't afford to replace a stolen bike. So, using the bike-sharing scheme gives me the speed I need to make my 9:00 AM lectures without the anxiety of locking up my own bike outside. It's cheaper than the bus and safer than owning a bike.

**4. Interviewer: Have you ever walked to a bike station and found it empty? How did that affect your plans?**
* **Eoin:** Yes, walking to a station only to find it empty is a major frustration. It's a problem because I need to know if a bike is available before I even leave my apartment.

**5. Interviewer: Have you ever arrived at your destination and found the bike station full? What did you do then?**
* **Eoin:** That happens, specifically near the campus engineering building. It forces me to cycle to a further station and walk back, which makes me even later.

**6. Interviewer: How does the Dublin weather influence your decision to rent a bike?**
* **Eoin:** It influences me a lot. If it's going to rain during my 20-minute ride, I will decide to take the bus instead. Getting caught in unforeseen rain showers is one of my big frustrations.

**7. Interviewer: Do you check a weather app before you leave?**
* **Eoin:** I try to. I really want that data integrated so I can make a "go/no-go" decision before I head out.

### Phase 3: Feature Validation
**8. Interviewer: Real-time Info: If you could see exactly how many bikes are left at a station from your phone, would that change how you plan your walk?**
* **Eoin:** Absolutely. My goal is speed and reliability; I need to know immediately if a bike is available at the nearest station before I leave my apartment.

**9. Interviewer: Journey Planning: When going somewhere new in Dublin, do you usually know the best cycling route, or do you use Google Maps?**
* **Eoin:** Since I have high tech literacy, I am comfortable with apps, but my main focus right now is my daily commute where I already know the route. My issue isn't directions, it's knowing if bikes will still be available in 10 minutes when I arrive.

**10. Interviewer: Parking Assurance: On a scale of 1 to 10, how important is it for you to know if there is an empty stand at your destination before you even start cycling?**
* **Eoin:** That is extremely important. I need to know if there is a free stand near the engineering building so I'm not late trying to dock the bike.
